Google guidelines

What helps a Google Business Profile — and what can hurt it.

Good local SEO is not about stuffing keywords or chasing shortcuts. This page keeps the important boundaries visible.

Good practice

  • Use the real-world business name.
  • Keep address or service-area information accurate.
  • Choose categories that genuinely describe the business.
  • Keep services, hours and other information current.
  • Use useful, relevant photos and updates.
  • Respond to reviews honestly and appropriately.
  • Keep the business owner in control of the profile.

Based on Google Business Profile representation and third-party guidance.

What we avoid

  • Adding extra keywords to the business name just to rank.
  • Fake, purchased or fabricated reviews.
  • Claiming a profile without the owner's authorization.
  • Promising a guaranteed #1 position.
  • Impersonating Google or suggesting we are Google.
  • Making changes without the business owner's consent.
  • Holding a profile or access hostage after a client ends the relationship.

These boundaries reflect Google's published Business Profile policies.

Ownership stays with you

You should retain ownership or co-ownership. A third-party manager can be given the appropriate access role.

Transparency matters

Google's third-party policy expects agencies to communicate changes and disclose management fees.

Ranking is not a guarantee

Optimization can improve the information and relevance of a profile, but no legitimate provider can promise a specific Google position.
